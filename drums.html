Days

A line is one whole day, midnight to midnight, the newest at the foot. Left to right is how often something comes round: once every 8h at the left, to once every 2m at the right. A line stands up, and its pen turns hot and wide, where the counts of that day rose and fell at that period more than chance makes them. A stripe that runs up through line after line, in the same place, is something arriving on that schedule day after day. Green is ordinary: decay alone leaves every line green.

Months

The same paper and the same pen over a longer axis: a line is one calendar month, from once every 10d at the left to once every 20m at the right. What a day does once a day is here, where it comes round thirty times: a line that stands up at 1d is something that rises and falls once a day.

Both papers are worked out from the log the counter has been keeping all along, a row every thirty seconds, every counter's rows together. A line is not asked about anything that has not had time to come round three times, and thirty seconds nobody counted are left out, not taken for thirty seconds of nothing.
